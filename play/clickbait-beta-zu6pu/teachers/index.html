Clickbait Campus: Teacher guide

A free browser game about phishing, sponsored content and checking the news with SIFT. One class period. No accounts, nothing collected.

What students do

Each student is a first-year at Goose State University with a laptop, a phone and a TV. They read short (silly) course readings and take quizzes, answer professors, dodge phishing emails and scam texts, notice ads dressed up as advice, and answer their parents' "Is this true?" questions about the news by searching and reading laterally. Grades and stress decide the semester; news answers decide whether the town ends up in a Golden Age or an Age of Rumors. The end screen debriefs every important choice with the lesson behind it, in tabs: School, Family & friends, News & research, and Scams & spam.

Learning goals: by the end, students can…

  1. Spot a phishing message by checking the sender's address, where a link really goes, urgency, generic greetings and requests for a password. Information Has Value
  2. Recognize sponsored content: search ads, "free trial" traps, affiliate links and scareware, and say who profits from each. Information Has Value SIFT: Stop
  3. Read laterally: leave a site to find out who runs it and what others say about it, before trusting it. Authority Is Constructed and Contextual SIFT: Investigate
  4. Compare coverage of one story across outlets and choose the most reliable account to pass on. Searching as Strategic Exploration SIFT: Find better coverage
  5. Trace a claim to the original document (meeting minutes, a study, a recall notice) and notice when a headline says more than the source does. Information Creation as a Process SIFT: Trace

Before class (5 minutes)

  • Play it once yourself first. Any up-to-date browser on a computer or Chromebook works.
  • Each student gets their own random mix of classes, emails, scams and news stories, so the debrief makes a good "what did you get?" conversation.
  • Students can turn music and sound off with the 🎵 and 🔊 buttons. To start everyone silent, add ?sound=off to the end of the link.

A 50-minute lesson

0–5Warm up. "Has a text or email ever almost fooled you? What gave it away?" Share the game link.
5–25Play. The tour, then a 15-minute semester. Tip: everyone can press Pause if you need the room.
25–40Debrief. Everyone opens the same debrief tab; use the questions below.
40–47Real world. Hand out the SIFT sheet. Try one move on a real headline from this week.
47–50Exit ticket. "One thing I'll check next time before I click or share."

Discussion questions

News & research

  • Which site did you send your parents, and why? What did "About this source" tell you that the site didn't?
  • Did anyone open the original document? What did it actually say, compared with the headlines?
  • The angriest headline was often the least reliable. Why might it still feel the most convincing?

School

  • Did you catch a professor's correction to a reading? What do you do when a source you trust gets something wrong?

Scams & spam

  • Which tells gave the phishing email away? Did anyone hover over a link first?
  • Some fake sites in the game show a padlock. What does a padlock really promise?
  • When a blogger links to a shop, who earns money if you buy? Does that change how you read the review?

The big picture

  • You could ace the semester and still end up in the Age of Rumors. Why does the game keep those separate?
  • How did one person's answers change what a whole town believed?
